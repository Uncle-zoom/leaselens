What surprised me most was how much the public record already reveals: one mailing address in New Haven connects 18 different owner names and 88 parcels, and one management company is listed as a principal on 239 registered companies. The biggest gap is housing conditions: Connecticut doesn't publish heat, mold or pest violations, so LeaseLens can only show street-level reports. With another month I would add New York City, where those violations are public, and test LeaseLens with a tenant union, the users who would benefit most. Building it taught me that an AI agent is only as trustworthy as the data and the checks around it; the most important code in LeaseLens isn't the AI, it's the fact check that removes anything the data can't support.

When I moved to New Haven for SOM, I arrived 3 days before class started because I was on active orders with the military. Choosing an apartment meant juggling Zillow, Google Maps, Reddit threads and city websites, and none of them answered the two questions that matter most: what is this block really like at night, and who will I actually be dealing with when something breaks? In a college town, many buildings are owned by LLCs with names like "311 Blake Development LLC," which tell a renter nothing. The records that connect those companies to the people and firms behind them are public, but they sit in state databases no renter has time to search.

As an intelligence analyst in the Army Reserve, I spend my time turning scattered open-source data into a clear picture. LeaseLens applies that same discipline to the biggest financial commitment most students and young professionals make each year: signing a lease.

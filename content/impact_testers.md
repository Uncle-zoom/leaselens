# Real tester feedback

Four people tried LeaseLens in October 2026. Their feedback is summarized below in
Deji's words (paraphrased from conversation, not verbatim quotes). No timing was measured.

## Tester 1: lives in downtown New Haven, near a nightclub
- Called LeaseLens a great tool and wished apartment listing sites offered this before you sign.
- Has the exact problem LeaseLens checks for: their apartment is close to a nightclub and a busy road,
  and they cannot sleep most nights without earplugs; there is a lot of road noise too.
- What it shows: the noise check (bars, nightclubs, rail lines and other noise sources within a short
  walk) answers the question this renter could not answer before signing.

## Tester 2: current renter
- Saw LeaseLens as a strong add-on for the large listing companies that already show apartments.
- Wants more cities, wherever the data exists.
- Could find little information about their own apartment before moving in. After meeting the
  neighbors, they learned that problems the neighbors had reported were never fixed.
- What it shows: renters want maintenance and complaint history before they sign, which is exactly
  the gap LeaseLens names in its limits (Connecticut does not publish housing-condition violations)
  and the reason New York City, where those records are public, is next on the roadmap.

## Testers 3 and 4: two current renters in the same downtown New Haven building
- Both live in the same downtown New Haven building on Chapel Street and have lived there since July.
- They tested LeaseLens on their own building. It listed 5 nightclubs, 15 bars, 8 pubs, 1 fire station,
  3 hospitals and 1 rail line within 800 m, and 112 street-level reports in the last 12 months within 200 m.
- They told Deji the noise findings matched the issues they have been experiencing since moving in in July.
- Checking their address also exposed a bug: a grocery 78 m away was routed as a 13-minute walk. It is fixed,
  with a test.
- One of them said: "I wish I had a tool like this before I moved in, I will be using this for my second year apartment hunt."
- Both agreed to be quoted without their names.

## What this evidence does and does not show
- Four testers confirmed the problem from their own experience; tester 1 wished listing sites offered
  this and tester 2 saw it as an add-on for the big listing companies.
- Two of the four (testers 3 and 4) checked the app against their own building, and said the noise
  findings matched their experience since July.
- No time saving is claimed: no timing was measured.
